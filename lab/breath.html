01.

The three modes

Hanaseh (4-2-6). Avin's signature ratio, drawn from Qadiri sitting practice in 2017. Slightly weighted toward the exhale; well suited for early morning sittings and evening winding-down. The shortest hold of the three.

4-7-8. Popularised in English by Andrew Weil; the older root is in pranayama. A larger hold makes the cycle slow and parasympathetic. Use for sleep onset or acute stress, not for working hours.

Box (4-4-4-4). The military / first-responder pattern. Symmetric, predictable, easy to learn. Useful as a baseline before exploring asymmetric ratios.

Note: If you feel light-headed at any point, stop. The breath is a practice, not a stunt. The first eight cycles of any new pattern are exploratory.

02.

Reading the visual

Inner orb. The breath body. Scales with the phase.

Mid-ring. Lags slightly. Represents the somatic delay between intention and sensation.

Corona. Drifts inward on inhale, outward on exhale. The signal of the inhale into the room, the exhale into the world.

Pin. The still centre. Always there.
