M / مدیتیشن

نشستن و سکوت

Sitting practice, drawn from the Qadiri Sufi tradition of Kurdistan, refracted through contemporary somatics. Twelve lessons. One year of slow study. Begin with twenty minutes; end with whatever is left of you.

Meditation, in this academy, is not a wellness technique. It is a discipline of attention with a four-hundred-year lineage in our part of the world, and a still older one to the east of us. We sit because the body remembers what the mind has forgotten. We sit because the cosmos does not respond to noise. We sit because, after a while, the act of sitting itself becomes the question.

The twelve lessons below are arranged in a slow ascending order, but they are not strict prerequisites for each other. Lessons one through three are open access. The remaining nine are reserved for students enrolled in the annual programme.

خواندنی‌های پیشنهادی

A short list, weighted toward the first weeks.

  • REF-01
    Annemarie Schimmel, Mystical Dimensions of Islam (1975)
    The standard introduction to the Sufi traditions. Read chapters 6 and 7 before lesson M-03.
  • REF-02
    Shunryu Suzuki, Zen Mind, Beginner's Mind (1970)
    Different lineage, identical instruction at the level of the cushion.
  • REF-03
    Christina Feldman, Boundless Heart (2017)
    A contemporary Theravada teacher reading the brahma-viharas with care.
  • REF-04
    Peter Levine, In an Unspoken Voice (2010)
    Somatic experiencing. Background reading for postural geometry (M-04).
03.

آهنگِ تمرین

هفته

Twenty minutes, six days

Same hour each morning. No exception, no apology for the missed seventh.

ماه

One long sit, forty minutes

On the new moon. With the door closed; with the phone in another room.

سه‌ماه

Half-day retreat, three hours

Solo, in nature if possible. At the equinox or the solstice. Without an agenda.

04.

بستن این شاخه

No-one closes meditation. The track ends, the practice does not. By M-12 the question shifts; we no longer ask whether we sat today. We ask what sitting has revised in the rest of the hours. That is the only real exam, and it is graded by the next twenty years.

"After the twelfth lesson, the student walks out of the room and the room walks out of the student." From the marginalia of an unnamed Qadiri sheikh, Sanandaj, 1972.
  1. The Qadiri (or Qadiriyya) order is a Sufi brotherhood founded by 'Abd al-Qadir al-Jilani in twelfth-century Baghdad. In Kurdistan it has long had a strong vernacular tradition that did not always sit comfortably with the urban schools.
  2. "Hanaseh" (هَناسه) is the Sorani word for breath. It is used throughout the academy as the name of our signature 4-2-6 breath cycle.
  3. For students enrolled in the full programme, lessons M-04 through M-12 unlock at fortnightly intervals across the year.
RHYTHM OF PRACTICE

Suggested cadence

DAILY

Ten minutes seated. Same chair, same time. No striving, no apps.

WEEKLY

One longer sit (30 minutes) on a weekend morning. No timer until afterwards.

QUARTERLY

A half-day silent practice each season. Walk, sit, eat, write. Phone off the whole time.
